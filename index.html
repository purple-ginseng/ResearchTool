Lux et Scientia

Open sourceMIT licence · free · no account

Your papers, notes and LaTeX in one local workspace.

An open-source reference manager, PDF reader with notes and Overleaf-style LaTeX editor in one app on your own computer, with your data in a plain folder. AI is optional and runs through your own Claude Code, Codex, Grok Build or Antigravity CLI.

Features

From a pile of PDFs to a paper

Reference library, PDF reader, notes and LaTeX in one workspace, in the four steps of a piece of research. Each step shows how it works, then the real screen; click any screenshot to enlarge it.

01 · Collect

Bring in the pile you have

Drop in PDFs, whole folders or a .bib file from Zotero, Mendeley or JabRef, or paste arXiv ids, DOIs, OpenReview and publisher links; duplicates are reported. Papers are organised into research lines, with sub-lines in shades of the line's colour once a line has 4 or more papers; with an AI CLI set up, new papers are filed for you in the background (on by default, and you can switch it off).

  • Import PDFs, folders and .bib (BibTeX) files, or paste arXiv ids, DOIs, OpenReview links
  • Full-text search over PDFs, notes and key points (Chinese too)
  • ⌘K / Ctrl+K “Go to anything”; venues verified with Crossref, DBLP and more

02 · Read

Read once, keep the page

Key points are the sentences that carry the paper, each linked to its page in the PDF, with your own remarks beside them. Select text in the PDF and “Quote in notes” puts it into your Markdown note as a quote with its page link, so every quote leads back to where it came from.

  • PDF search, contents, clickable internal links, colour highlights
  • Markdown notes with formulas, pictures, videos and version history
  • Sentence-level key points, figures found locally, export as .md (opens in Obsidian, Typora or VS Code) or a .zip package

03 · Connect

See where the ideas come from

Lineage shows the works a research line builds on and how your papers cite them, from OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar, as a History line on a time axis or as Ancestry. The Related tab lists one paper's references and the papers citing it; a lookup sends only the paper's DOI or arXiv id.

  • Editable method diagrams; practice quizzes, flash cards and spaced daily review
  • References, citing papers and a Lineage citation graph (OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar)
  • Comparison tables of up to 15 papers, every cell with its pages

04 · Write

From note to \cite to PDF

Cite from your library with the \cite pickers and completion; references.bib is regenerated from the library with only the entries the project cites. latexmk compiles on your computer into a live PDF preview, with SyncTeX in both directions.

  • Live PDF preview with latexmk, clickable log, SyncTeX both ways
  • Cite from your library; references.bib holds only what you cite
  • Every save is a version; optional Overleaf sync over Git

Optional

AI with your own CLI and account

AI features run one CLI on your computer, signed in with your own account: Claude Code by default, or OpenAI Codex, xAI Grok Build or Google Antigravity. The app calls no AI API itself and never stores an API key; without an AI CLI, library, reader, notes and editor still work fully.

What it does: explanations and translations with page citations, note drafts, rewrites, method diagrams, quizzes and comparison tables.

Local-first

Your research stays on your computer

No account and no cloud. Here is exactly what stays local and what goes online.

Diagram: your computer runs ResearchTool on 127.0.0.1, with a workspace folder of PDFs, notes, LaTeX projects and library.json. Only these leave it: lookups you start, which send a paper's id, DOI, link or title; optional Overleaf sync, which pulls and pushes a LaTeX project to git.overleaf.com through your own git; and, if you set one up, your own AI CLI signed in with your own account, which then also files newly imported papers in the background by default. Requests from other machines and web sites are refused.

Stays local

PDFs, notes, highlights, key points and LaTeX projects live in one plain folder. The server listens on 127.0.0.1 only and refuses requests whose Host or Origin is not this local address.

Online only for lookups, sync and the AI

Lookups happen only when you use a feature that needs them, and send only a paper's id, DOI, link or title. Overleaf sync, if you use it, goes to git.overleaf.com through your own git. Every host it can contact is listed below.

AI (optional)

ResearchTool calls no AI API itself and holds no API key or password. What your CLI sends to its provider falls under your own account with that provider. Once a CLI is set up, filing newly imported papers runs in the background by default; you can switch it off.

Exactly what stays local, and which hosts are contacted

Stays local

  • PDFs, notes, highlights, key points and LaTeX projects live in one plain folder (workspace/)
  • The server listens on 127.0.0.1 only; other machines and web sites cannot reach it
  • Fonts and scripts are bundled, never loaded from a CDN; no telemetry, no analytics
  • An undoable trash and a daily snapshot of the whole workspace

Online only for lookups, sync and the AI

Adding a paper by id, Related, Lineage, metadata and venue checks send only a paper's arXiv id, DOI, link or title (a venue check also gives Crossref the first author's surname), to:

  • arxiv.org
  • doi.org
  • api.crossref.org
  • api.openalex.org
  • api.semanticscholar.org
  • dblp.org
  • openreview.net
  • proceedings.mlr.press

When a paper is added, its PDF is downloaded from arXiv, OpenReview or the open-access copy OpenAlex names on a publisher or repository site. Answers are cached in workspace/cache, venue checks are kept in workspace/venues.json. “Find links” also asks arXiv and huggingface.co (only the arXiv id is sent) and visits the project and code links a paper names; Overleaf sync talks to git.overleaf.com through your own git.

AI (optional)

  • ResearchTool calls no AI API itself and holds no API key or password
  • AI features run your local CLI (claude, codex, grok or agy); what it sends to Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI or Google falls under your own account with that provider
  • Comparison tables send only the passages that match the columns, never the whole PDF, and show a token estimate first; for a first draft Claude reads the paper's PDF pages, the other CLIs get its extracted text
  • Auto-filing and briefs of newly imported papers are on by default: they send the title, tags and up to 350 characters of the text to your AI CLI in the background; switch them off in “Group with Claude”
  • No account
  • No telemetry
  • Works offline (except lookups, Overleaf sync and the AI)

Get started

Run it locally: three commands, no install step

The server uses only Node's standard library and the browser libraries are bundled, so there is no npm install and no build. It runs on macOS, Linux (Ubuntu) and Windows 10 / 11 with the same commands.

  1. 1

    Clone and start

    Terminal
    • git clone https://github.com/purple-ginseng/ResearchTool.git
    • cd ResearchTool
    • node server.js
  2. 2

    Open it in your browser

    http://localhost:4790

    Your data goes to ./workspace (created on the first start). To keep it elsewhere or run a second copy, on macOS / Linux: RT_WORKSPACE=~/ResearchTool-data PORT=4791 node server.js; in Windows PowerShell: $env:RT_WORKSPACE="$HOME\ResearchTool-data"; $env:PORT=4791; node server.js

  3. 3

    Take the tour

    “Take the tour” on the Overview, or Help › Take the tour, shows the whole app in a few minutes.

Installing the tools on your system

ResearchTool runs on macOS, Linux (Ubuntu) and Windows 10 / 11, and its tests run on all three for every push to main and every pull request. The server starts each tool directly, never through a shell.

macOS

  • poppler: brew install poppler
  • TeX: MacTeX (or BasicTeX plus latexmk)
  • git comes with the Xcode command line tools: xcode-select --install
  • Spelling uses the system dictionary

Ubuntu / Debian

  • sudo apt install poppler-utils latexmk texlive-latex-extra unzip git
  • For spelling, a word list too: sudo apt install wamerican
  • The in-page folder browser replaces the native macOS folder chooser
  • Grok Build needs Linux kernel 5.13 or newer

Windows 10 / 11

  • poppler: scoop install poppler, choco install poppler or conda-forge
  • TeX: TeX Live, or MiKTeX plus a Perl (such as Strawberry Perl), with MiKTeX's “install missing packages” set to Yes or No, not Ask
  • Git for Windows: for Overleaf sync, and needed by the native claude CLI
  • AI: Claude Code, Codex and Antigravity work on Windows; Grok Build does not (its read-only sandbox exists only on macOS and Linux)
  • Spell check is off (Windows has no system word list); the other writing checks work

FAQ

Questions, answered honestly

Is it free?

Yes. ResearchTool is open source under the MIT licence, with no paid tier and no sign-up. Only outside services you choose to use (such as the AI CLI you pick, or Overleaf's Git integration, which is a Premium feature) have their own terms.

Does it need Claude or another AI?

No. Without an AI CLI the library, reader, notes, Related, Lineage and the LaTeX editor all work. The AI features (diagrams, quizzes, explanations, comparison tables, note drafts, auto-filing) need one AI command-line tool installed and signed in on your computer: Claude Code by default, or OpenAI Codex, xAI Grok Build or Google Antigravity.

Which AI tools can I use, and can they change my files?

Pick one in Preferences › AI, signed in with your own account: Claude Code (claude, the default), OpenAI Codex (codex), xAI Grok Build (grok, macOS and Linux only) or Google Antigravity (agy 1.5.13 or later; a personal Google account works). Gemini CLI is not offered. ResearchTool never asks for an API key and never stores one.

In the AI panel, Claude Code can edit files. Codex can only if you tick “Let Codex edit files” (off by default), and then only the open LaTeX project and your reading notes; library.json is copied before each such run, and every project change is kept in the history and can be restored. Grok Build and Antigravity are read-only. The AI features themselves (diagrams, tables, drafts) only read. Only Claude reads PDF pages itself; the other CLIs get the extracted text.

Can I bring my library from Zotero, Mendeley or JabRef?

Yes, through BibTeX: export a .bib file from your current reference manager and import it, along with PDFs or whole folders; pasted BibTeX, arXiv ids, DOIs and OpenReview or publisher links work too, and duplicates are reported. Comment headings or JabRef groups in a .bib can regroup the library. The other way, Export references writes references.bib, notes.md and library.csv, and the Markdown of a paper package (.zip) opens in Obsidian, Typora or VS Code.

Does it work with Overleaf?

Yes, through Overleaf's own Git integration, which is an Overleaf Premium feature: import an Overleaf project, then pull and push from the editor; if both sides changed the same lines you choose which version wins. The token stays in git's credential store, and ResearchTool never sees your Overleaf password. Compiling happens on your computer with latexmk (pdfLaTeX, XeLaTeX or LuaLaTeX), with SyncTeX both ways. ResearchTool is a workspace for one person, with no real-time co-editing.

Can it help with a literature review?

The Related tab lists a paper's references and the papers citing it (OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar), each added in one click; Lineage shows the prior, derivative and similar works of a paper, a research line or the whole library, in the spirit of Connected Papers. With an AI CLI you can also fill a comparison table of up to 15 papers with page references, ask one question of all of them, or draft related work in LaTeX from your notes that cites only papers in your library. AI output is a draft to check against the papers.

Where is my data?

In workspace/ next to the app (or wherever RT_WORKSPACE points): library.json, the PDFs, Markdown notes, LaTeX projects and their history, all as plain files. Deleted things go to a trash first, and Settings can keep daily snapshots on another disk.

Which systems does it run on?

macOS, Linux (Ubuntu) and Windows 10 / 11. The tests run in CI on all three for every push to main and every pull request, PDF import with poppler included.

A few things differ: on Linux and Windows the in-page folder browser replaces the native macOS folder chooser (on Windows, “This PC” leads to other drives); spell check uses the system word list (wamerican on Debian / Ubuntu) and is off on Windows, which has none; Overleaf sync keeps its token in git's credential store (the macOS Keychain, the Windows Credential Manager or a Linux helper such as libsecret). The install commands for each system are under Get started above.

Of the AI CLIs, Grok Build does not work on Windows; Claude Code, Codex and Antigravity do. ResearchTool is a personal project, shared as it is. If something does not work on your system, an issue describing what happened is very welcome.

Is there a Chinese interface?

Yes. Preferences › General › Display language switches between English and 中文, and the guided tour is translated too. Full-text search handles Chinese, and the language of AI drafts and of category names has its own settings.

Does it work offline?

Yes. All browser libraries and fonts ship with the app. Only adding papers by id, Related, Lineage, metadata and venue checks, Find links, Overleaf sync and the AI features need a connection.
